Privacy Policy

Twenty-Two Dots · Last updated: October 19, 2026

Twenty-Two Dots is a YouTube channel of voiceless, physics-simulated football Shorts. Its videos are published by Twenty-Two Dots Uploader, a private tool used only by the channel's operator to upload videos to the Twenty-Two Dots channel and to read how they perform. It is not offered to anyone else, and it collects nothing about the people who watch the channel.

Google user data the uploader accesses

With the operator's consent through Google sign-in, the uploader uses YouTube API Services on the operator's own channel only:

  • YouTube Data API: uploads the channel's videos and sets them to private or scheduled, reads those videos' details and status back to confirm every setting was applied, pulls a scheduled video back to private when its match is moved, and, at sign-in and before each upload, reads the signed-in account's own channel to confirm it is the Twenty-Two Dots channel.
  • YouTube Analytics API: reads aggregate reports for the channel's own videos, such as views, watch time and audience retention, to plan future videos.

Where the data is kept

The sign-in token and any analytics reports are stored only on the operator's own computer, in files readable by the operator's user account alone. There is no server and no database, and nothing is kept about any other Google account.

Sharing and use

  • Data received from Google APIs is never sold, shared or transferred to anyone.
  • It is never used for advertising, and never used to train AI models.
  • Twenty-Two Dots Uploader's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

YouTube and Google

The uploader uses YouTube API Services. Using it means agreeing to the YouTube Terms of Service, and Google's handling of data is described in the Google Privacy Policy. Watching Twenty-Two Dots videos on YouTube is governed by YouTube's own policies.

Revoking access

The uploader's access can be revoked at any time from the Google Account security settings, under Third-party apps and services. Revoking stops all access immediately, and the stored token stops working.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted here with an updated date.
